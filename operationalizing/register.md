**The apport.** `register: know | do | govern` — **derived, never stored**.

**What it operationalizes.**
A coarse navigational handle over the force.
Humans and tooling want a three-way grouping — *reference / how-to / decisions* — but the theory offers no such primitive; it offers the force's **direction of fit** and (via `constitutive`) its markedness.
`register` is the readable name we compute from those.

**The derivation.**

- `constitutive: yes` ⇒ `govern` (the act posits or fixes a social object).
- otherwise, the force's direction of fit ⇒ `know` (word→world: orient, explain, describe) or `do` (world→word: instruct, teach, recommend).

So the third bin is not a third direction of fit — it is the constitutive one.
The three `writing/forces/` doors stay; only their *foundation* changes.

**Why derived, not stored.**
Storing `register` would double-encode what `force` + `constitutive` already fix, and drift from them.
So it is generated for display and grouping, like `diataxis` and the register-ordinal — one of the "compute, never store" coordinates.

**Alternatives considered.**
Store it — rejected (redundant, drifts).
Make it binary `{know, do}` and drop `govern` — rejected: the maintainer keeps the familiar three-way handle, re-founded rather than removed.
Treat `govern` as a third direction of fit — rejected: that was the ill-founded original.

**The work it does.**
Orders the force templates into three doors; groups a function's sub-shelf; gives readers and generators a coarse filter.

The derivation itself is implemented in the IT tools (it is computed, not written) — this fiche states the rule, not the code.

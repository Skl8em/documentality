`orient` situates the reader in a whole. It is **formative** — its job is to change the reader's cognitive map (`intention: locate`), not to inform a task. Use [`template.md`](template.md) to write one.

- **Generative verb:** *situate.*
- **Stake:** the reader never loses the thread. Every element answers one of four questions — *what is this, where am I, why does it concern me, where do I go next* — and anything answering none is noise.
- **Move structure:** name the whole in one line → locate the reader within it → state relevance to their likely purpose → route onward to the right next document.
- **Failure mode:** turning into a manual (drifting to `describe`) or a tutorial (drifting to `teach`). An orientation that tries to be complete has failed — its job is to hand off, not to hold.
- **Organize by the reader**, not the subject: model where the reader actually stands and what they need next, not where the map says they are.
- **Genres:** README, project overview, landing page, annotated table of contents, "start here" page.

Recipient note: an `orient` usually travels far in `distance` (newcomers share little code) and carries no `power`, so scaffold generously and keep armor light. See [`../../../patterns.md`](../../../patterns.md).

`mandate` institutes a binding norm.
It sits **at the floor** (`intention: state`): it serves by fixing an obligation, it does not try to move the reader.
It is the top notch of the do-door deontic gradient — *here is how* → *you should* → *you must* — but lives behind **govern & record** because it institutes a norm rather than helping a reader act.
Use [`template.md`](template.md) to write one.

- **Generative verb:** *require.*
- **Stake:** the unambiguous boundary between obligatory and recommended.
  A convention that leaves the reader unsure whether a rule is binding has failed at its one job.
- **Move structure:** state the rule plainly → fix its scope (who, when, where it binds) → give the rationale, but briefly, lest it read as optional → state the consequence of non-compliance.
- **Failure mode:** softening into advice — into `recommend` or `explain`.
  The modals weaken from *must* to *should*, and the reader can no longer tell what is required.
- **Genres:** policy, naming / coding convention, standard, `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

Recipient note: a `mandate` is placed by the **domain it governs**, not by its register (ADR-025): a rule binding the *user's* writing is product content; the same force binding *contribution to this project* is `steering/CONTRIBUTING.md`.
The `decide` that installed it is a separate, frozen record the rule cites.

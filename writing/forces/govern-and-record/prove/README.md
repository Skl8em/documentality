`prove` establishes that a claim holds, reproducibly.
It is a **suasive** force (`intention: suasive`, the *convince* aim): its instrumental aim is to bring a reader who does not already trust you to accept the claim as established.
Use [`template.md`](template.md) to write one.

- **Generative verb:** *demonstrate.*
- **Stake:** that the evidence *covers* the claim and the demonstration is reproducible.
  Showing results without the method proves nothing to a reader who does not already trust you.
- **Move structure:** state the claim precisely → declare the method → exhibit the evidence → establish coverage (sufficient for the claim, not merely consistent with it) → state the limits of what was shown.
- **Failure mode:** the gap between "consistent with" and "sufficient for" — where most proofs quietly fail.
- **Genres:** audit report, test report, validation dossier, compliance evidence.

Recipient note: the sharpest case is `prove` toward an authority — `distance` *and* `power` at maximum: expose the whole method *and* be airtight against hostile reading.
That cost is the structure of `prove` rewritten by the two coordinates at their extreme, and is why audit dossiers are expensive; do not pay it where it is not owed.

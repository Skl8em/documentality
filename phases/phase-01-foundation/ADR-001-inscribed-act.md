## Status

accepted — 2026-07-11

## Context and forces in play

Use-centered frameworks (Diátaxis foremost) describe only documents a user consults to learn or operate a system. They have no place for decisions, commitments, proofs, proposals, accounts, conventions — most of what an organization actually writes. We needed an organizing principle broad enough to cover all of it.

## Options considered

- **Extend Diátaxis** with extra quadrants — consequence: ad hoc, no principled closure, still user-centered.
- **Classify by subject/topic** — consequence: the failure mode of every wiki; a document serves many subjects.
- **Classify by the act performed** (speech-act theory) — consequence: principled (direction of fit gives a closure), covers the full repertoire, and the label *generates* the writing.

## Decision

A document is a **typified communicative act, inscribed from a definite position, that performs an illocutionary `force` toward a definite recipient**. The force is the primary coordinate, and naming it correctly generates how to write the document.

## Entail — what follows

- The whole system is organized around forces, not topics or user-needs.
- We inherit a philosophical debate (whether the illocutionary classes are truly closed) — accepted as "saturated, not closed."
- Diátaxis is not discarded but re-derived as a projection (see ADR-004).
